Health officials respond to viral claim that a new vaccine contains tracking microchips

By Jane Coteau · Updated July 7, 2026 · 4 min read
[ Article image ]
A widely shared social post prompted responses from public health agencies this week.

A claim circulating on social media this week alleged that a newly approved vaccine contains microchips capable of tracking recipients. The post, shared tens of thousands of times, offered no evidence and cited no named sources.

Public health experts and independent fact-checkers have described the claim as false. Vaccines do not contain microchips, tracking devices, or any technology capable of transmitting a person's location, according to multiple health agencies.

To help readers judge the credibility of the original post, our newsroom ran it through an independent verification service. The result appears in the panel on the right.

Officials reiterated that vaccine ingredients are publicly listed and reviewed by regulators before approval, and encouraged the public to rely on primary sources when evaluating health claims online.
